Audit Scope & Baseline Sources: This audit evaluates how much of the APC 2023 presidential manifesto — Renewed Hope 2023: Action Plan for a Better Nigeria (RENEWED-HOPE_compressed.pdf, 80 PDF pages; indexed in tinubu_manifesto_notes_01_2023_action_plan.md) — was accomplished between inauguration on May 29, 2023 and November 13, 2026 (roughly 41.5 months, or 86.5% through the four-year constitutional term ending May 29, 2027). 1. Methodology, Attribution Standards & Classification Taxonomy

1.1 Why No Arbitrary Percentage Score or Partisan Grade Is Assigned

Reducing an 80-page governing manifesto to an arbitrary percentage score or letter grade masks profound structural differences across pledges:

  1. Unequal Scope & Materiality: Enacting a single statutory bill (e.g., the Judicial Office Holders Salaries and Allowances Act, 2024) is far narrower in macro-welfare footprint than hitting a 10% GDP growth path to $780.9 billion, halving youth unemployment, or lifting health insurance to 40% of 220M+ citizens. Treating every bullet point as equal in a naive percentage average distorts performance.
  2. Methodological Breaks & Rebasing (2023–2026): During 2023–2026, Nigeria’s National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) overhauled three flagship yardsticks: (a) adopting the ILO 1-hour/week labour force definition (which lowered headline unemployment mathematically from 33.3% to 4.3%–5.3% while high informality persisted); (b) rebasing GDP to 2019 prices (₦205.09T 2019; ₦372.82T 2024); and (c) rebasing the Consumer Price Index (CPI) to 2024 in January 2025 (which reset headline inflation from 34.80% in December 2024 to 24.48% in January 2025). Direct pre-2023 vs. 2026 comparisons require explicitly noting these statistical breaks.
  3. Elapsed Interim Deadlines vs. Full-Term 2027 Horizon: Several 2023 manifesto pledges set hard interim deadlines that have already elapsed (6 months for PIA/deepwater incentives = Nov 2023; 24 months for 1M ICT jobs & 2 years for 40% NHIA = May 2025; 2025 for 90% broadband), whereas multi-year targets (4 years for 65% arable land & halving youth unemployment; 2027 for 2.6 mbpd crude, +20% gas, OB3/AKK; 2030 for 5-min potable water) sit near or beyond November 2026.

Instead of a partisan grade, this audit disaggregates 101 distinct, concrete, measurable pledges, quantitative targets, institutional reforms, and policy doctrines across all 16 policy chapters (PDF pp. 7–74, using strict PDF Page = Printed Page + 2 pagination) and classifies each transparently under one of five categories, triangulating official/primary data (CBN, NBS, DMO, NUPRC, NERC, NRS, FMINO, State House) against independent/critical sources (World Bank, IMF, SBM Intelligence, Amnesty International, Transparency International, BudgIT, Premium Times, The Punch, Vanguard, BusinessDay, FIJ, PLAC).

1.2 Five-Category Classification Definitions

Category Operational Criteria
Implemented / Achieved Enacted, operationalised, or substantially achieved (~85–100%) as promised, verified by primary records.
Partially Implemented / In Progress Concrete statute, agency, funding, or physical construction launched and advancing, but material coverage gaps, missed interim deadlines, or incomplete delivery remain as of November 13, 2026.
Mixed / Disputed Outcome Substantive executive/legislative action taken, but accompanied by sharp policy contradictions against the 2023 text, severe unintended social/macro trade-offs, corruption/operational disruptions, or sharply divergent official vs. independent assessments.
Not Achieved / Stalled / Reversed Missed an elapsed hard deadline without delivery, stalled with minimal execution, remained un-enacted/unbuilt, or experienced an explicit doctrinal policy reversal (e.g., CBN rate hikes, pure FX float, stopping Anchor Borrowers, suspending Nigeria Air, Niger exiting MNJTF).
Insufficient Verifiable Data Official and independent public disclosures through Nov 13, 2026 lack audited, transparent metrics needed to verify fulfillment or non-fulfillment.

1.3 Broad Aspirations vs. Measurable Targets

Rhetorical commitments in the Foreword (PDF pp. 4–6) and Closing Statement (pp. 75–76) — e.g., "build a Nigeria... where sufficient jobs with decent wages create a better life", "no parent is compelled to send a child to bed hungry", "a nation of creators, not just of consumers", and "obliterate terror, kidnapping, banditry" — function as overarching political vision statements rather than discrete auditable deliverables. Appendix A catalogues these broad aspirations separately and explains how they map onto the 101 measurable pledges audited below.
