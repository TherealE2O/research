## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & INVESTIGATIVE SCOPE

This dossier investigates the hidden human supply chain underlying global artificial intelligence systems and social media moderation. 

While multinational technology companies market Artificial Intelligence as automated, self-governing, and socially responsible, these systems depend on human workers performing content moderation and data labeling under extreme psychological stress. Major technology corporations—including Meta Platforms (Facebook, Instagram) and OpenAI—have outsourced this traumatic labor to Kenya and other African nations to exploit cheap labor, high youth unemployment, and permissive regulatory environments.

This report synthesizes primary reporting from **HumAngle Media** and **TIME Magazine**, verified court records from the Employment and Labour Relations Court of Kenya, and filings before the Court of Appeal at Nairobi.

## 2. SECTION I: THE HUMANGLE INVESTIGATION — NIGERIAN RECRUITS IN NAIROBI

On October 8, 2026, investigative outlet **HumAngle Media** published a major report titled *"Africans Hired to Moderate Facebook Content Left With Sleepless Nights"*, documenting deceptive cross-border recruitment targeting educated Nigerian youth.

### 2.1 The Case of Kauna Malgwi
* **Background:** Raised in Borno State, Northeast Nigeria—a region experiencing over a decade of Boko Haram terrorist insurgency and massacres—Kauna Malgwi relocated to Nairobi, Kenya, to complete her Master's degree in Clinical Psychology.
* **The Deceptive Recruitment:** In 2019, she was recruited by outsourcing contractor **Sama** under the representations that she was accepting a position in "Hausa linguistic translation and localization".
* **The Reality:** Upon arriving in Nairobi and executing strict Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs)—some of which carried explicit threats of up to 20 years' imprisonment for disclosure—she was assigned to Meta's graphic moderation operations. Her daily duties consisted of reviewing raw video feeds of Boko Haram terrorist executions, beheadings, suicide bombings, and torture originating from her home region.
* **Psychiatric Consequences:** Exposure to violent footage from the very insurgency she had fled triggered severe trauma, persistent insomnia, and clinical panic disorders. Following her exit in 2023, Malgwi founded the **Digital Rights and Mental Health Initiative (DRMHI)** and became a lead organizer of the African Content Moderators Union.

### 2.2 The Case of Mustafa Mukhtar
* **Recruitment:** Recruited from Nigeria under expectations of customer service and linguistic support.
* **The Breakdown of Counseling:** Mukhtar testified that in-house "wellness counselors" provided by Sama were so undertrained and overwhelmed by the volume of trauma that the therapists themselves broke down in tears when moderators described the graphic material they were processing.

* **Primary Verification Link:** [HumAngle Media Investigation: "Africans Hired to Moderate Facebook Content Left With Sleepless Nights"](https://humanglemedia.com/africans-hired-to-moderate-facebook-content-left-with-sleepless-nights/)

## 3. SECTION II: FACTORY CONDITIONS — THE 50-SECOND DEATH CLOCK & SUB-$2 WAGES

Investigations by HumAngle and TIME Magazine documented severe operational pressures inside the Nairobi moderation facility:

1. **The 50-Second Quota:** Moderators were required to analyze, categorize, and apply complex policy determinations to graphic video and image tickets within an arbitrary limit of **50 seconds per item**. Continuous surveillance via keystroke loggers and automated activity trackers penalized workers whose accuracy dropped below 95%.
2. **Sub-$2 Hourly Compensation:** African moderators were paid between **40,000 to 60,000 Kenyan Shillings per month (approximately $300 to $450 USD)**, representing an hourly wage of **$1.50 to $2.20 per hour**. Content moderators in North America and Western Europe performing identical tasks earn between $18 and $25 per hour.
3. **81% Clinical PTSD Rate:** Formal psychiatric evaluations of 144 content moderators conducted by independent medical experts and submitted to the Kenyan High Court in December 2024 revealed that **81% of moderators suffered from severe clinical Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)**, alongside acute rates of chronic depression, anxiety, and insomnia.

* **Primary Verification Link:** [TIME Magazine: "Inside Facebook's African Sweatshop" by Billy Perrigo](https://time.com/6147458/facebook-africa-content-moderation-employee-treatment/)

## 4. SECTION III: CORPORATE ARCHITECTURE — SUBCONTRACTING AS A LEGAL SHIELD

Multinational technology corporations maintain distance from operational liabilities through offshore subcontracting agreements. Meta Platforms contracted San Francisco-based outsourcing provider **Sama** (headquartered locally as Samasource Kenya UB) to operate its content moderation hub in Nairobi.

When workers suffered mental health crises and brought legal action, Meta argued in Kenyan court proceedings that:
1. Meta Platforms is a foreign legal entity incorporated in Delaware with principal offices in California, outside the territorial jurisdiction of Kenyan courts.
2. The workers were direct employees of third-party contractor Sama, not Meta.
3. Employees had executed legally binding Non-Disclosure Agreements and dispute resolution arbitration clauses.

## 5. SECTION IV: CONNECTED SCANDALS — OPENAI / CHATGPT & THE ETHIOPIAN CIVIL WAR LAWSUIT

The labor practices documented at Sama extend beyond social media moderation to generative artificial intelligence and regional conflict zones:

### 5.1 The OpenAI / ChatGPT Toxic Data Labeling Scandal (TIME Magazine, Jan 2023)
In 2021 and 2022, prior to the public launch of ChatGPT, OpenAI contracted Sama in Nairobi to read, categorize, and label tens of thousands of text snippets extracted from the internet depicting child sexual abuse, bestiality, suicide, and sexual violence to build safety guardrails for AI models.

Kenyan data labelers were paid between **$1.32 and $2.00 per hour** to process explicit traumatic content. Sama canceled the contract early after workers reported acute psychological distress.

* **Primary Verification Link:** [TIME Magazine: "Exclusive: OpenAI Used Kenyan Workers on Less Than $2 Per Hour to Make ChatGPT Less Toxic"](https://time.com/6247678/openai-chatgpt-kenya-workers/)

### 5.2 The Tigray War & The Murder of Professor Meareg Amare ($2 Billion Lawsuit)
During the civil war in Ethiopia (2020–2022), Meta maintained fewer than 30 content moderators covering over 100 million people across Amharic, Oromo, and Tigrinya languages at the Nairobi hub. In November 2021, Professor Meareg Amare of Bahir Dar University was targeted by viral hate speech posts on Facebook falsely accusing him of treason and publicizing his residence. Despite multiple formal reports submitted to Facebook by his son, Abrham Meareg, Meta’s automated and human systems failed to remove the posts. Professor Meareg was followed and murdered outside his residence.

In December 2022, Abrham Meareg, Ethiopian human rights researcher Fisseha Tekle, and the Katiba Institute filed a **$2 Billion (250 billion KSh) lawsuit** against Meta in the Kenya High Court, alleging that Facebook's recommendation algorithm promoted violent ethnic conflict.

* **Primary Verification Link:** [The Guardian UK: "Facebook owner Meta sued in Kenya over Ethiopia violence and hate speech"](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2022/dec/14/facebook-owner-meta-sued-kenya-ethiopia-violence-hate-speech)

## 6. SECTION V: THE JUDICIAL LANDMARK — KENYAN COURT OF APPEAL RULINGS

The legal resistance against Big Tech in Africa began with South African whistleblower **Daniel Motaung**, who filed *Petition No. E070 of 2022* in the Kenya Employment and Labour Relations Court after being fired for union organizing.

In *Meta Platforms, Inc. & Meta Platforms Ireland Ltd v. Daniel Motaung & Others* (Civil Appeal No. E186 of 2023), the Kenyan Court of Appeal delivered a landmark ruling in September 2024 rejecting Meta’s appeal seeking immunity from local courts. The appellate judges established that **foreign multinational corporations operating within the digital economy of African nations are subject to the jurisdiction of domestic courts** regarding labor exploitation and human rights violations committed within the territory.

The litigation, supported by UK-based justice organization **Foxglove Legal** and Kenyan legal advocates, is currently seeking **$1.6 Billion in compensation, healthcare trust funds, and systemic labor reforms** for over 1,100 affected moderators.
