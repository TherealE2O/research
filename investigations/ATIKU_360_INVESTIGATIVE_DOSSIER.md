## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & INVESTIGATIVE SCOPE

This dossier constitutes a comprehensive, forensic investigation into the public career, commercial holdings, family dynasty, international legal probes, and 30-year political ambition of **Atiku Abubakar (GCON)**, the 11th Vice President of the Federal Republic of Nigeria (1999–2007) and six-time presidential contender.

No political figure in Nigeria's Fourth Republic has straddled state power, international commerce, and party politics with the endurance and controversy of Atiku Abubakar. Across five decades, Atiku evolved from an orphaned Fulani boy in British Northern Cameroons to a Cadet Customs Officer, an oil logistics billionaire, the architect of Nigeria's 2001 telecommunications revolution, the constitutional bulwark that dismantled Olusegun Obasanjo's Third Term bid, and the perennial presidential candidate whose refusal to concede power rotation in 2022 ultimately shattered the opposition coalition.

Every finding in this report is anchored in primary documentation: United States Senate investigative transcripts, Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) affidavits, Supreme Court of Nigeria law reports, National Assembly committee inquiries, and corporate registry disclosures.

## 2. SECTION I: ORIGINS, JADA TRUST TERRITORY & THE 1961 UN PLEBISCITE CITIZENSHIP CHALLENGE

### 2.1 Birth and Paternal Tragedy
Atiku Abubakar was born on **November 25, 1946**, in Jada, Adamawa State. His father, **Garba Atiku Abdulkadir**, was an itinerant Fulani scholar and merchant from Wurno, Sokoto, who opposed Western education. In 1954, Garba was briefly incarcerated by Native Authority police in Ganye for refusing to register Atiku in primary school. In **1957**, Garba drowned while attempting to cross the rain-swollen Mayo Choncha river. His body was interred on the riverbank, leaving eleven-year-old Atiku as an only surviving child to be raised by his mother, **Aisha Kande**.

### 2.2 The 1961 UN Plebiscite & The Constitutional Citizenship Battle
At the time of Atiku's birth in 1946, Jada was part of **British Northern Cameroons**, a United Nations Trust Territory administered by the United Kingdom separate from the Colony and Protectorate of Nigeria.

On **February 11–12, 1961**, the United Nations conducted a plebiscite wherein Northern Cameroons voted (146,296 to 97,659) to join independent Nigeria, taking effect on June 1, 1961.

In the 2019 and 2023 presidential election petitions, the All Progressives Congress (APC) challenged Atiku's qualification under **Section 131(a) of the 1999 Constitution**, asserting that because Jada became Nigerian territory only in 1961, Atiku (born in 1946) was not a "citizen of Nigeria by birth."

* **Judicial Resolution:** In *Atiku Abubakar & PDP v. INEC, Bola Ahmed Tinubu & APC* (SC.348/2023), the Supreme Court of Nigeria dismissed the petition, ruling that under the 1961 Northern Cameroons Plebiscite Order-in-Council and Section 25(1) of the 1999 Constitution, all residents of Northern Cameroons who incorporated into Nigeria on June 1, 1961, acquired unimpaired constitutional citizenship by birth.

## 4. SECTION III: 20-YEAR CUSTOMS CAREER & THE 1984 "53 SUITCASES" MMIA BREACH

Joining the Department of Customs and Excise on **June 30, 1969**, Atiku served for two decades across Idi-Iroko border, Apapa Quays, Ibadan, Kano, Maiduguri, and Kaduna, before rising to Area Comptroller of Murtala Muhammed International Airport (MMIA) and voluntary retirement as **Deputy Director of Customs** on April 30, 1989.

### The April 1984 MMIA 53 Suitcases Breach
During General Muhammadu Buhari's nationwide currency change and total border closure, the **Emir of Gwandu (Haruna Jokolo)**, father of Buhari’s Aide-de-Camp **Major Mustapha Jokolo**, landed at MMIA from Saudi Arabia with 53 suitcases.

Armed presidential guards led by Major Jokolo took physical control of the baggage area, intimidated customs personnel, bypassed mandatory currency declarations and rummaging, and loaded the 53 uninspected suitcases into military vehicles. While Atiku reported the breach up the civil service hierarchy, no customs officer was penalized, and Major Jokolo later maintained the baggage held personal family gifts. The incident remains a textbook historical symbol of military impunity over civilian customs controls.

## 5. SECTION IV: COMMERCIAL EMPIRE — GABRIELE VOLPI & THE ONNE PORT MONOPOLY

In 1982, while serving as a senior customs officer, Atiku partnered with Italian-Swiss businessman **Gabriele Volpi** and Major-General Shehu Musa Yar'Adua to found **NICOTES (Nigeria Container Services)**, later rebranded as **INTELS Nigeria Ltd** under offshore parent entity **Orlean Invest Holding**.

* **The Onne Port Monopoly:** INTELS developed the swamp creeks of Onne, Rivers State into the dedicated Oil and Gas Free Zone, securing an exclusive concession to handle oil-service vessels.
* **The 28% NPA Commission:** INTELS collected all marine service boat charges across eastern pilotage districts on behalf of the Nigerian Ports Authority (NPA), retaining a **28% agency commission**.
* **Constitutional Conflict of Interest:** Critics argued this equity holding violated **Section 2(b), Part I of the Fifth Schedule of the 1979 Constitution**, forbidding public officers from private business other than farming. Atiku maintained he was merely an "investor and shareholder," not an active operational manager.
* **Divestment (2018–2020):** Following NPA regulatory terminations under the Buhari government, Atiku completely sold his residual family stake in Orlean Invest for over **$100 million**.

## 6. SECTION V: PDM APPRENTICESHIP & THE 1993 JOS SDP PRIMARY DEAL

Under Major-General Shehu Musa Yar'Adua, Atiku helped build the **Peoples Democratic Movement (PDM)**. At the March 1993 Jos SDP presidential primary, after Abiola (3,617 votes), Kingibe (3,255 votes), and Atiku (2,066 votes) forced a runoff, Yar'Adua directed Atiku to step down for M.K.O. Abiola on the condition that Atiku would be named Vice President. While Abiola won the runoff, SDP governors forced him to pick Babagana Kingibe instead. Following Yar'Adua's death in Abakaliki prison in 1997, Atiku inherited the supreme leadership of the PDM machine.

In 1998, Atiku won the Adamawa State governorship before Olusegun Obasanjo drafted him as PDP Vice-Presidential nominee.

## 8. SECTION VII: THE 2003 "NEAR-COUP" & OBASANJO WAR

At the January 2003 PDP primaries, 21 PDP governors led by James Ibori urged Atiku to trigger the "Mandela Option" (restricting Obasanjo to one term). Obasanjo was compelled to visit Atiku's lodge late at night to plead for support. Atiku backed Obasanjo, who won 2,642 to 661 over Alex Ekwueme. However, the humiliation spurred Obasanjo to wage a full-scale institutional war against Atiku during their second term.

## 9. SECTION VIII: KILLING THE THIRD TERM AGENDA (MAY 2006)

When Obasanjo loyalists introduced a constitutional amendment to extend presidential tenure to three terms, Atiku openly revolted. On April 5, 2006, he convened opposition lawmakers in Abuja, working with Senate President **Ken Nnamani** and House Speaker **Aminu Masari**. On **May 16, 2006**, the Senate killed the Third Term bill via a decisive voice vote on live national television.

## 10. SECTION IX: THE PTDF MUTUAL COUNTER-DOSSIERS

In 2006, Obasanjo and the EFCC accused Atiku of diverting $145M in Petroleum Technology Development Fund (PTDF) deposits to Trans International Bank and Equitorial Trust Bank to benefit business associates (Otunba Fasawe and Mike Adenuga/Globacom).

In reply, Atiku published detailed bank drafts proving that Fasawe’s Mofas account was a joint PDP slush fund that funneled ₦100M to Obasanjo’s Ota farm, purchased ₦200M in Peugeot vehicles for the presidency, and funded constructions at Obasanjo's private Bells University of Technology.

## 11. SECTION X: THE WILLIAM JEFFERSON $100K FBI FREEZER STING

In 2005, US Congressman William J. Jefferson (D-LA) solicited money from FBI undercover informant Lori Mody, claiming a $100,000 cash payment was required to bribe "the Vice President of Nigeria" (Atiku) for an iGate/NITEL broadband contract.
* On August 3, 2005, the FBI raided Jefferson's New Orleans home and found **$90,000 in marked bills in his kitchen freezer**.
* Simultaneously, the FBI raided Atiku's private residence in Potomac, Maryland.
* Jefferson was convicted in 2009 and sentenced to 13 years in federal prison. The US Department of Justice never charged Atiku, as wiretaps established that Jefferson kept the money himself.

## 12. SECTION XI: THE 2010 US SENATE LEVIN REPORT ($40M WIRES)

In February 2010, the US Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, chaired by **Senator Carl Levin**, released *Keeping Foreign Corruption Out of the United States: Four Case Histories* (S. Hrg. 111-597).
* The 330-page report documented how Atiku and his wife, Jennifer Douglas, brought **over $40 million in suspect offshore funds** into US accounts between 2000 and 2008 via shell vehicles (*LetsGo Ltd.*, *Guernsey Trust*) and $2.8 million in Siemens AG bribe kickbacks.
* The report led to an informal decade-long US visa ban under Presidential Proclamation 7750, finally broken in January 2019 when Atiku stayed at the Trump International Hotel in DC on a temporary waiver.

## 13. SECTION XII: 30-YEAR SERIAL CANDIDACY & THE 2022 ZONING BREACH

Atiku launched six presidential campaigns: 1993 (SDP), 2007 (AC), 2011 (PDP), 2015 (APC), 2019 (PDP), and 2023 (PDP).

In May 2022, following Buhari’s 8-year northern rule, Atiku breached the PDP's southern zoning pact, defeating Nyesom Wike at the party convention. This triggered the **G5 governors' revolt** (Wike, Makinde, Ortom, Ikpeazu, Ugwuanyi), while Peter Obi defected to the Labour Party and Kwankwaso took Kano.

* **The Electoral Math:** The fractured opposition won a combined **14.58 million votes** (Atiku: 6.98M; Obi: 6.10M; Kwankwaso: 1.50M)—dwarfing Bola Ahmed Tinubu's winning tally of **8.79 million votes**. Atiku's refusal to step down for a southern candidate fractured the anti-APC vote and handed Tinubu the presidency.
