## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & FORENSIC CHRONOLOGY (2007–2023)

### The Investigative Premise
Between May 2007 and May 2023, Bola Ahmed Tinubu executed the most sophisticated, high-stakes political survival and power-accumulation strategy in post-colonial African history. Beginning in May 2007 as a lone opposition governor-emeritus in Lagos State—surrounded on all sides by the federally dominant Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) that controlled 28 of Nigeria's 36 states—Tinubu transformed from a regional chieftain into a master political kingmaker, the primary architect of Nigeria's first successful political party merger, the chief enabler of Muhammadu Buhari’s historic 2015 presidency, and ultimately the victor of the tumultuous 2023 presidential election.

This dossier documents the sixteen-year political chess game across five distinct historical epochs:
1. **The Opposition Fortress (2007–2011):** The rebranding of Alliance for Democracy (AD) into Action Congress (AC) and Action Congress of Nigeria (ACN), followed by a meticulously funded legal-forensic assault that overturned PDP governorships in Edo, Ekiti, and Osun, and reclaimed Ogun and Oyo through the ballot box.
2. **The Grand Merger (2013–2014):** The bridge across Nigeria's historic ethno-regional fault line, uniting the Yoruba Southwest (ACN), the Hausa-Fulani core North (CPC), the traditional Northern/Eastern opposition (ANPP), and disaffected PDP renegades (nPDP) into the All Progressives Congress (APC).
3. **The 2015 Watershed:** The primary victory at Teslim Balogun Stadium, Tinubu's strategic withdrawal from the vice-presidential ticket in favor of his legal protégé Prof. Yemi Osinbajo SAN, and the historic unseating of incumbent President Goodluck Jonathan.
4. **The Cold War & The Cabal (2015–2022):** The systemic marginalization of Tinubu by the "Aso Rock Cabal" (Mamman Daura, Abba Kyari, Babachir Lawal, Isa Funtua), the Bukola Saraki National Assembly rebellion of 2015, the June 2020 Villa dissolution of the Adams Oshiomhole National Working Committee (NWC), and the late 2022 / early 2023 Godwin Emefiele Naira Redesign crisis viewed as targeted economic sabotage.
5. **The Climax: "Èmi L'ókàn" & The 2023 Triumph:** The defiant June 2, 2022 Abeokuta declaration, the defeat of the Villa-backed "consensus candidate" plot (Ahmad Lawan), the crushing primary victory at Eagle Square, the high-stakes Muslim-Muslim ticket with Senator Kashim Shettima, and the contested February 25, 2023 election victory affirmed by the Supreme Court on October 26, 2023.

## SECTION 1: EXPANDING THE OPPOSITION STRONGHOLD (2007–2011)

### 1.1 The Genesis: From AD to Action Congress (AC) and ACN
In the 2003 general elections, President Olusegun Obasanjo led a federal electoral blitzkrieg across the Southwest, capturing five of the six states previously held by the Alliance for Democracy (AD)—Oyo, Ogun, Osun, Ondo, and Ekiti. Bola Ahmed Tinubu survived as the sole opposition governor in Lagos State. 

Realizing that AD was structurally immobilized by ideological puritanism and gerontocratic control by the Afenifere leadership (Chief Abraham Adesanya, Chief Ayo Adebanjo, Sir Olaniwun Ajayi), Tinubu initiated a two-phase structural transformation:

#### The Internal Schism and Birth of the AC (2006)
- **The Split:** The AD fractured into two factions: the conservative Afenifere-backed faction led by Chief Michael Mojisoluwa Akinfenwa, and the reformist, pro-Tinubu faction led by Chief Bisi Akande.
- **The Vehicle:** In September 2006, Tinubu joined forces with disaffected elements from the ruling PDP—most notably Vice President Atiku Abubakar’s faction, the Advanced Congress of Democrats (ACD), alongside politicians like Chief Audu Ogbeh, Chief Tom Ikimi, and Alhaji Abubakar Rimi—to launch the **Action Congress (AC)**.
- **The Platform:** Atiku Abubakar ran as the AC presidential candidate in April 2007 following his bitter fallout with Obasanjo. Though Atiku lost in an election universally condemned by domestic and international observers for systemic rigging, the AC established itself as the preeminent opposition vehicle.
- **The 2010 Rebranding:** In August 2010, at a national convention in Benin City, Edo State, the party rebranded as the **Action Congress of Nigeria (ACN)**. The explicit purpose was to shed the regional Southwest label and build a pan-Nigerian political identity capable of challenging the PDP nationwide.

Following the heavily flawed April 14, 2007 gubernatorial elections conducted by Professor Maurice Iwu's Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), the PDP held power in every Southwest state except Lagos, as well as Edo State in the South-South. 

Tinubu did not accept the status quo. Instead, he pioneered an unprecedented legal-forensic machine. He established a legal war room in Lagos, financing high-powered legal teams led by Nigeria's foremost Senior Advocates of Nigeria (SANs) and introducing **forensic biometric ballot paper analysis** into Nigerian election jurisprudence.

#### Detailed Breakdown of the Tribunal Battles

#### 1. Edo State: Adams Oshiomhole vs. Prof. Oserheimen Osunbor
- **The Election (April 14, 2007):** INEC declared Prof. Oserheimen Osunbor (PDP) winner with 329,740 votes against Adams Oshiomhole (AC) with 166,577 votes.
- **The Tribunal Challenge:** Oshiomhole, backed by Tinubu's legal apparatus, challenged the result across 12 Local Government Areas (LGAs), alleging widespread ballot-box stuffing, non-accreditation, and falsification of Form EC8A/EC8B.
- **The Tribunal Judgment (March 20, 2008):** The Edo State Election Petition Tribunal, chaired by Justice Ahmed Badamasi, nullified thousands of illegal votes credited to the PDP. It recalculated the lawful votes: Oshiomhole scored 166,577 votes against Osunbor’s 129,017 votes, declaring Oshiomhole the validly elected Governor.
- **Appellate Affirmation (November 11, 2008):** The Court of Appeal in Benin City, led by Justice Umaru Abdullahi (President of the Court of Appeal) alongside a 5-member panel, delivered a unanimous judgment upholding the tribunal’s decision. Osunbor was sacked from office, and Oshiomhole was sworn in the following day. This gave Tinubu's AC its first foothold outside the Southwest in the resource-rich South-South.

#### 2. Ekiti State: Dr. Kayode Fayemi vs. Segun Oni (The 42-Month War)
- **The Election (April 14, 2007):** INEC declared Engr. Segun Oni (PDP) winner with 177,689 votes against Dr. Kayode Fayemi (AC) with 108,305 votes.
- **First Tribunal & Court of Appeal Rerun Order (February 17, 2009):** After the initial tribunal dismissed Fayemi's petition, the Court of Appeal in Ilorin nullified Oni's election in 10 out of 16 LGAs and ordered a rerun election.
- **The Rerun Crisis & Ayoka Adebayo Drama (May 2009):** The rerun in Ido-Osi LGA descended into chaos. INEC Resident Electoral Commissioner (REC), Mrs. Ayoka Adebayo, dramatically went into hiding, submitting a resignation letter citing pressure to act against her "Christian conscience." Following intense federal pressure, she returned to office and declared Oni winner.
- **The Second Tribunal (May 5, 2010):** The retrial tribunal delivered a split 3–2 judgment upholding Oni’s election (Justice Hamma Barka presiding).
- **The Historic Appellate Judgment (October 15, 2010):** A 5-member panel of the Court of Appeal in Ilorin, presided over by Justice Ayo Salami (with the lead judgment written by Justice Clara Bata Ogunbiyi), delivered a blistering verdict. The court deducted all fraudulent votes from Ido-Osi and other disputed units. Final tally: Fayemi scored 105,631 valid votes against Oni’s 95,176 votes. Oni was ousted, and Fayemi was sworn in as Governor of Ekiti State.

#### 3. Osun State: Engr. Rauf Aregbesola vs. Prince Olagunsoye Oyinlola
- **The Election (April 14, 2007):** INEC declared incumbent Prince Olagunsoye Oyinlola (PDP) winner with 385,039 votes against Rauf Aregbesola (AC) with 243,457 votes.
- **The First Tribunal & The "Justice Thomas Naron" Call-Log Scandal:** Aregbesola petitioned the election across 10 LGAs. The first tribunal, chaired by Justice Thomas Damina Naron, dismissed the petition. However, investigative media (*The News* magazine and *Sahara Reporters*) published forensic mobile phone call-logs between Justice Naron and Olagunsoye Oyinlola’s lead counsel, Kunle Kalejaiye SAN. The records revealed hundreds of private calls and SMS text messages exchanged while the tribunal was actively sitting:
  > *"The phone logs proved direct, collusive communication between the presiding judge and the defense counsel during the formulation of tribunal rulings."*
- **Judicial Consequences:** Following a petition to the National Judicial Council (NJC), Justice Thomas Naron was investigated, found guilty of gross judicial misconduct, and dismissed from the bench in February 2013. Kunle Kalejaiye SAN was subsequently tried before the Legal Practitioners Disciplinary Committee (LPDC).
- **The Retrial & The Landmark Judgment (November 26, 2010):** The Court of Appeal ordered a retrial. When the second tribunal led by Justice Ali Garba also ruled against Aregbesola, the matter reached the Court of Appeal in Ibadan. On November 26, 2010, a 5-member panel chaired by Justice Clara Bata Ogunbiyi nullified the election of Oyinlola. The court accepted the biometric forensic evidence prepared by Adrian Forty’s team, cancelled the fabricated results in 10 LGAs, and declared Aregbesola the duly elected Governor of Osun State with 198,799 lawful votes against Oyinlola’s 172,880.

#### 4. Oyo and Ogun States: The 2011 Ballot Box Conquest
Having built momentum through the courts, Tinubu’s ACN swept the April 2011 general elections:
- **Ogun State:** Senator Ibikunle Amosun (ACN) won 284,814 votes, crushing both the PDP candidate (Chief Adetunji Olurin, 188,698 votes) and Governor Gbenga Daniel’s splinter People’s Party of Nigeria candidate (Gboyega Nasir Isiaka, 137,974 votes).
- **Oyo State:** Senator Abiola Ajimobi (ACN) scored 275,147 votes, defeating incumbent PDP Governor Adebayo Alao-Akala (212,572 votes) and former Governor Rashidi Ladoja of Accord (147,119 votes).

By May 2011, Bola Ahmed Tinubu controlled **five out of the six Southwest states** (Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Osun, Ekiti), while his ally Adams Oshiomhole held Edo State. Ondo State remained under Dr. Olusegun Mimiko (Labour Party), who maintained cordial working ties with Tinubu's axis. Tinubu stood as the undisputed political emperor of Southwest Nigeria.

### 1.3 The Judicial Cold War: Justice Ayo Salami vs. CJN Aloysius Katsina-Alu
The systematic recovery of Southwest states by the ACN ignited panic inside the ruling PDP national leadership and the Presidency under Goodluck Jonathan. The PDP accused the Court of Appeal, headed by Justice Isa Ayo Salami, of operating a "judicial conspiracy" with Bola Ahmed Tinubu.

#### The Sokoto Gubernatorial Petition Clash
In late 2010, a ferocious conflict erupted between the Chief Justice of Nigeria (CJN), Justice Aloysius Katsina-Alu, and Court of Appeal President, Justice Ayo Salami:
- **The Interception:** Salami publicly alleged that CJN Katsina-Alu directed him to "arrest" (halt) the judgment of the Court of Appeal Sokoto Division in the gubernatorial petition between Muhammadu Dingyadi (DPP) and Aliyu Wamakko (PDP), in order to save the PDP governor from being sacked.
- **The Supreme Court Intrusion:** Katsina-Alu caused the Supreme Court to intervene in an interlocutory appeal on the Sokoto case, despite election petition appeals for governorships terminating constitutionally at the Court of Appeal at that time.
- **The Retaliation and Salami's Suspension:** Salami refused to compromise and deposed to an affidavit detailing the CJN's interference. In response, the National Judicial Council (NJC), controlled by Katsina-Alu loyalists, investigated Salami, accused him of perjury for refusing to apologize to the CJN, and suspended him in August 2011. President Goodluck Jonathan promptly approved Salami's suspension.
- **Significance:** The Salami affair demonstrated the extent to which Tinubu’s tribunal victories had shaken the Nigerian state architecture. The removal of Salami was seen as the PDP's desperate attempt to close the appellate courthouse door to Tinubu’s expanding opposition empire.

## SECTION 2: THE HISTORIC 2013–2014 APC MERGER

### 2.1 The Post-2011 Strategic Re-evaluation: The Math of Opposition Defeat
In the April 2011 presidential election, President Goodluck Jonathan (PDP) polled 22,495,187 votes, comfortably defeating:
- General Muhammadu Buhari (Congress for Progressive Change - CPC): 12,214,853 votes (almost entirely concentrated in the Northwest and Northeast).
- Mallam Nuhu Ribadu (Action Congress of Nigeria - ACN): 2,079,151 votes (failing to secure even the majority of the Southwest vote, as Jonathan swept 5 of 6 SW states).
- Mallam Ibrahim Shekarau (All Nigeria Peoples Party - ANPP): 917,012 votes.

#### Why the 2011 Alliance Talks Collapsed
In March and April 2011, intense negotiations were held at Tinubu's Bourdillon residence in Ikoyi, Lagos, between the ACN and Buhari’s CPC:
1. **Structural Mismatch:** The ACN wanted a formal merger with cross-ticket integration; the CPC wanted an eleventh-hour loose electoral pact.
2. **The Presidential Flagbearer Deadlock:** Neither party would cede the top ticket. Ribadu insisted on staying in the race; Buhari refused to withdraw.
3. **The Vice-Presidential Dispute:** Buhari had already selected Pastor Tunde Bakare (Save Nigeria Group) as his running mate. The ACN insisted that if Buhari took the ticket, an ACN nominee must replace Bakare. Buhari refused to drop Bakare days before the deadline.
4. **Mutual Distrust:** ACN leaders feared CPC’s Northern insularity; CPC leaders suspected Tinubu was bargaining behind the scenes with Jonathan.

#### The Lesson of 2011
Following the 2011 defeat and the post-election violence in northern states, Tinubu and Buhari arrived at an inescapable mathematical reality:
$$\text{Northern Talakawa Grassroots (CPC)} + \text{Southwest Financial/Organizational Engine (ACN)} + \text{Traditional Northern/Eastern Base (ANPP)} = \text{Defeat of the PDP Monolith}$$

Individually, each opposition party was a regional nuisance. Unified, they formed an unassailable national majority.

In March 2013, as the merger documents were being finalized, an obscure, unregistered association calling itself the **"African Peoples Congress"** applied to INEC for registration using the identical acronym **APC**.

### 3.2 The Vice-Presidential Sacrifice: The Genesis of Osinbajo

Having delivered the presidential ticket to Buhari, Tinubu believed he was entitled to the vice-presidential slot, creating a Buhari-Tinubu ticket.

#### The Veto of the Muslim-Muslim Ticket
- **The Internal Rebellion:** Severe internal opposition erupted within the APC leadership against a Muslim-Muslim ticket in a country split roughly 50/50 between Christians and Muslims:
  - Senator Bukola Saraki wrote articles and organized Northern Christian and moderate Muslim leaders, warning that a Muslim-Muslim ticket would alienate Southern Christians and hand reelection to Jonathan.
  - Rivers Governor Rotimi Amaechi, who served as Director-General of the Buhari Campaign, strongly advised Buhari that a Muslim-Muslim ticket was commercially unviable in the South-South, Southeast, and Middle Belt.
  - General Buhari himself, sensitive to past allegations of religious fanaticism, told Tinubu that he could not run with a fellow Muslim.
- **Tinubu's Strategic Concession:** Tinubu relented, realizing that clinging to the ticket would tear the fledgling alliance apart. However, he insisted on the absolute prerogative to choose Buhari’s Christian running mate.
- **The Nominating Formula:** 
  According to Tinubu's own historical account during his Abeokuta address on June 2, 2022:
  > *"Buhari gave me three slots: Yemi Osinbajo, Wale Edun, and Yemi Cardoso. I said no, I would not give you three names so you don't choose who you like. I submitted only one name: Professor Yemi Osinbajo."*

#### Who was Yemi Osinbajo?
Professor Yemi Osinbajo SAN was Tinubu’s Attorney-General and Commissioner for Justice in Lagos State (1999–2007). He was a Senior Advocate of Nigeria, a respected academic, a Senior Pastor at the Redeemed Christian Church of God (RCCG)—Nigeria’s largest Pentecostal denomination—and a completely loyal legal technician with zero independent political base. By putting forward Osinbajo, Tinubu neutralized Christian fears while placing his closest intellectual confidant a heartbeat away from the presidency.

### 4.1 The June 9, 2015 National Assembly Rebellion: The First Strike

Within days of Buhari’s inauguration on May 29, 2015, the battle for control of the 8th National Assembly erupted.

#### Tinubu's Blueprint:
- **Senate President:** Senator Ahmad Lawan (Yobe North - Northeast).
- **Deputy Senate President:** Senator George Akume (Benue North-West - North Central).
- **Speaker, House of Reps:** Rt. Hon. Femi Gbajabiamila (Surulere, Lagos - Southwest, Tinubu's foremost legislative protégé).
- **Deputy Speaker:** Rt. Hon. Mohammed Monguno (Borno - Northeast).

#### The Saraki-Dogara Ambush (June 9, 2015):
1. **The ICC Diversion:** The APC party leadership summoned all APC senators to an emergency reconciliation meeting with President Buhari at the **International Conference Centre (ICC)** in Abuja at 9:00 AM.
2. **The Chamber Seizure:** While Tinubu, party chairman John Odigie-Oyegun, and dozens of pro-Lawan senators waited at the ICC for Buhari (who never showed up), Senator Bukola Saraki, accompanied by a handful of APC renegades and all 49 PDP senators, walked into the National Assembly chamber.
3. **The Unopposed Election:** Finding a quorum present, Senator Ahmed Sani Yerima nominated Bukola Saraki. With the pro-Lawan senators trapped at the ICC, Saraki was elected Senate President unopposed.
4. **The PDP Deal:** In exchange for the PDP's 49 bloc votes, Saraki ceded the Deputy Senate Presidency to **Senator Ike Ekweremadu (PDP, Enugu West)**—installing the opposition in the nation's number two legislative office.
5. **The House of Representatives:** In the lower green chamber, Rt. Hon. Yakubu Dogara, in open alliance with the PDP caucus, narrowly defeated Tinubu's protégé Femi Gbajabiamila by 203 votes to 192 votes to become Speaker.

Tinubu was humiliated. The National Assembly leadership was now in the hands of actors who openly defied his authority and were actively aligned with the Aso Rock Cabal to checkmate his influence.

### 4.2 Sidelining and the 2016 Ondo Gubernatorial Crisis

Following the National Assembly coup, the Cabal systematically froze Tinubu out of federal governance:

Ministers from the Southwest—such as Babatunde Fashola (Works, Power & Housing), Kayode Fayemi (Solid Minerals), and Adebayo Shittu (Communications)—were appointed directly by Buhari without Tinubu's clearance. Fashola and Fayemi had grown increasingly independent of Tinubu's political godfathering.
- **The Hollow Title:** When journalists questioned Tinubu’s authority, presidential spokesmen clarified that "National Leader" was merely an informal, honorary title with zero statutory or constitutional executive authority.

#### The 2016 Ondo State Primary Explosion
The proxy war erupted into the open during the September 2016 Ondo State APC gubernatorial primary:
- Tinubu backed **Dr. Olusegun Abraham**.
- The party chairman, Chief John Odigie-Oyegun, supported by the Cabal and federal ministers, engineered the primary to favor **Rotimi Akeredolu SAN**.
- When the party’s internal Appeal Panel recommended the cancellation of the primary due to doctored delegates' lists, Oyegun unilaterally overruled the panel and submitted Akeredolu’s name to INEC.

Oyegun ignored the demand. Akeredolu went on to win the Ondo governorship with full federal backing from the Villa, proving that Tinubu could be defeated within his own Southwest backyard.

#### Aisha Buhari’s Whistleblowing (October 2016)
In an explosive BBC Hausa interview in October 2016, First Lady Aisha Buhari confirmed everything Tinubu’s camp had whispered:

### 4.3 The Rise and Fall of Adams Oshiomhole (2018–2020)

Recognizing that Oyegun was a willing tool of the Cabal, Tinubu bided his time until the June 2018 APC National Convention. Mobilizing governors and state chairmen, Tinubu successfully blocked Oyegun’s tenure elongation and installed his ferocious ally, former Edo Governor **Adams Oshiomhole**, as APC National Chairman.

#### Oshiomhole's Iron-Fist Orthodoxy
Oshiomhole launched a brutal enforcement of party supremacy, directly confronting powerful governors who opposed Tinubu’s hegemony:
- **Ibikunle Amosun (Ogun State):** Amosun sought to impose Adekunle Akinlade as his successor; Oshiomhole disqualified Akinlade and handed the APC ticket to Tinubu's candidate, Prince Dapo Abiodun.
- **Rochas Okorocha (Imo State):** Okorocha sought to install his son-in-law, Uche Nwosu; Oshiomhole denied Nwosu the ticket and handed it to Senator Hope Uzodimma.
- **Godwin Obaseki (Edo State):** Oshiomhole disqualified sitting Governor Obaseki from the APC primary on certificate discrepancies, prompting Obaseki to defect to the PDP and win reelection.

#### The June 25, 2020 Villa Coup: Sacking Oshiomhole
By mid-2020, anti-Tinubu governors, led by Kayode Fayemi (Ekiti), Rotimi Amaechi (Rivers), and Abubakar Atiku Bagudu (Kebbi), coordinated with the Cabal to eliminate Oshiomhole.
- **The Suspension:** An Abuja High Court suspended Oshiomhole following a factional ward crisis in Edo State, an order affirmed by the Court of Appeal on June 16, 2020.
- **The Giadom Faction:** Chief Victor Giadom declared himself Acting National Chairman and called an emergency National Executive Committee (NEC) meeting.
- **The Coup in the Council Chambers:** On **June 25, 2020**, President Buhari presided over the virtual NEC meeting inside the Presidential Villa. Without prior debate, Buhari approved the total **dissolution of the Adams Oshiomhole-led National Working Committee (NWC)**.
- **The Installation of Mai Mala Buni:** Buhari appointed **Governor Mai Mala Buni of Yobe State** as Chairman of the Caretaker/Extraordinary Convention Planning Committee (CECPC).

Tinubu’s control over the party machinery was liquidated with the stroke of a presidential gavel.

### 5.4 The High-Stakes Muslim-Muslim Ticket

- **Christian Association of Nigeria (CAN):** Strongly condemned the ticket, warning that running two Muslims in a polarized nation was an existential threat to secular stability.
- **Internal Revolt:** Former SGF Babachir David Lawal and former House Speaker Yakubu Dogara broke ranks with Tinubu, leading a revolt of Northern APC Christians and endorsing opposition candidates (Peter Obi and Atiku Abubakar).

#### The Cold Realpolitik Calculation:
Tinubu made an unapologetic calculation:
1. **The Northern Electoral Arithmetic:** The PDP had fielded Alhaji Atiku Abubakar (a Northern Fulani Muslim), and Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso (NNPP) controlled the massive Kano electorate.
2. If Tinubu selected a Northern Christian running mate (who represent demographic minorities in core Northern states), the Hausa-Fulani Muslim electorate would abandon the APC and vote en masse for Atiku or Kwankwaso.
3. Shettima provided heavyweight political stature, intellectual gravitas, and deep connections across the Northeast and Northwest Kanuri/Hausa political networks.
4. Tinubu cited the precedent of the **June 12, 1993 presidential election**, where Chief Moshood Abiola and Ambassador Babagana Kingibe ran on an all-Muslim Social Democratic Party (SDP) ticket and secured a historic national victory.
