## 2. Core Framing, Overarching Thesis & Strategic Architecture

### 2.1 Overarching Thesis: "From Reforms to Prosperity" (Agenda 2027–2031)
- **Two-term continuity narrative:** President Tinubu's foreword (p. 7) and Chapter intro (p. 16) frame the 2023–2026 first term as the period of difficult structural stabilisation—"removing the fuel subsidy to secure our financial future, unifying the exchange rate to encourage domestic production, and reforming tax policies to ensure a fairer distribution of responsibility" (p. 7)—asserting that *"The first term rescued the economy from old instabilities and rebuilt the foundations. The second term will turn stronger foundations into visible results in every state, local government, ward, and community"* (p. 16).
- **Household-centred prosperity test:** The manifesto states that *"Our central promise for 2027-2031 is national prosperity: an economy whose progress is felt not only in reforms, but in the homes, markets, farms, factories and workplaces of Nigerians every day"* (p. 16) and that *"macroeconomic recovery"* must translate into *"measurable household prosperity"* (p. 101)—specifically *"higher purchasing power and lower living costs"*, *"the food on our tables, the power in our homes, the security of our families, and the dignity of meaningful work"* (p. 7).
- **Consolidation from 8 Priorities into 4 Pillars ("Renewed Hope Agenda 2.0"):** Rather than discarding the 2023 *Eight-Point Renewed Hope Agenda* (pp. 8–9, 17), the 2027 manifesto consolidates its presentation into **four strategic delivery pillars** (pp. 2, 9, 17):
  1. **Pillar 1 — Secure and Resilient Nation** *(advances RHA Priority #2: Strengthen National Security for Peace and Prosperity)* (pp. 9, 17, 20).
  2. **Pillar 2 — Better Quality and More Affordable Life for Every Nigerian** *(advances RHA Priority #3: Boost Agriculture to Achieve Food Security, and Priority #6: Education, Health and Social Investment)* (pp. 9, 17, 34).
  3. **Pillar 3 — Energy, Infrastructure & Economic Growth** *(advances RHA Priority #1: Reform the Economy; Priority #4: Energy & Natural Resources; Priority #5: Infrastructure & Transportation; and Priority #7: Economic Diversification via Industrialisation, Digitalisation, Creative Arts, Manufacturing, and Innovation)* (pp. 9, 17, 53).
  4. **Pillar 4 — Effective and Accountable Governance** *(advances RHA Priority #8: Improve Governance for Effective Service Delivery)* (pp. 9, 17, 81).

### 2.2 Four Foundations, Federalism & Delivery Governance
- **Four Enduring Foundations (p. 17):** *"macroeconomic stability, the rule of law, institutional capability, and national cohesion."*
- **Fiscal Federation Delivery Model (pp. 13, 15):** Emphasises co-delivery across all three tiers and executive/legislative arms: Federal Executive leads/sets framework; National Assembly *"Co-Author, Not Spectator"* enables through statute and appropriation; 36 State Governors + FCT & State Assemblies deliver schools, hospitals, roads, state power markets, policing laws; 774 LG Chairmen & Councillors deliver primary health, wards, community services (`"Federal Leads and Delivers | NASS enables | States deliver | Councils deliver"`, p. 15).
- **Four Delivery Disciplines (p. 93):**
  1. *"Fund it first from what we already lose"* — recover and protect public value (oil revenue, electricity billing, project completion) so progress rests less on new taxes and borrowing.
  2. *"Complete before we begin"* — *"Repair before replacing, complete before starting afresh, prevent before treating, process before exporting, and measure outcomes rather than expenditure."*
  3. *"Put a name, a date and a number on every commitment"* — every commitment gets a baseline, target, cost, owner, and reporting date in a public delivery tracker.
  4. *"Let citizens keep the score"* — public dashboards and independent verification.
- **Eight Headline Outcomes Expected by 2031 (p. 18):** (1) Safer lives and communities; (2) Lower living costs & food security (inflation down, post-harvest losses halved); (3) Reliable power scaled towards 10,000 MW; (4) Stronger industry, trade & investment (zone hubs, 1m export-linked youth jobs by 2030); (5) Expanded jobs & higher real incomes (6% annual GDP growth, youth unemployment halved, credit to 50% of economically active Nigerians); (6) Quality education & healthcare (out-of-school children halved, 20,000 more teachers, 40% health insurance, ≥90% functional PHCs); (7) Affordable homes & secure land (towards 200,000 homes); (8) Responsive & accountable governance (digital case management to magistrate courts, published LG allocations).

## 3. Exhaustive Sector-by-Sector Catalog of 2027–2031 Promises, Policies & Targets

### 3.1 National Security, Defence, Policing, Borders, Corrections, Emergencies & Identity
- **Armed Forces Expansion & Operations (pp. 26, 31):**
  - Complete establishment and equipping of **4 new Nigerian Army divisions** in **Makurdi, Ilorin, Jalingo, and Benin City**; recruit, train, and deploy **28,000 additional approved personnel**, with public deployment reporting (pp. 26, 31).
  - *"Sustain intelligence-led kinetic operations against insurgent and kidnapping networks to degrade and dismantle their operating bases, backed by independent verification of reported operational outcomes to build public trust"* (p. 26).
  - Strengthen surrender and rehabilitation frameworks with standardised, verifiable reporting on all screened, reintegrated, and prosecuted ex-combatants (p. 26).
  - Establish specialised zonal counter-terrorism capabilities and fortify physical security across schools, markets, places of worship, and vital transport corridors (p. 26).
  - Expand peace-building, trauma support, and deradicalisation programmes to safely resettle internally displaced families (p. 26).
- **Policing, Civil Defence & State/Community Policing (pp. 18, 27, 31):**
  - Train and deploy **50,000 newly recruited police officers** and expand recruitment/training *"towards a 1:400 police-to-citizen ratio"* (pp. 27, 31).
  - Launch full implementation of the **Harmonised Police Reform programme**, deploying digital record systems, geographic mapping tools, **officer body cameras**, emergency dispatch channels, and forensic infrastructure nationwide (p. 27).
  - Transfer static private-protection duties to a fortified **Civil Defence service**, starting with recruitment of **30,150 approved personnel**, freeing police for frontline duty (pp. 27, 31, 105).
  - *"Work with the National Assembly, State Houses of Assembly, and governors to enact a constitutional and legal framework for accountable state and community policing, maintaining strict national standards and citizens' rights"* (p. 27).
- **Borders, Farms, Forests, Mines & Critical Infrastructure (pp. 28, 31, 94):**
  - Complete e-surveillance coverage across **all 190 mapped border areas** (closing the remaining **31 of 190** gaps); expand **e-Gates**, **APIS** (Advance Passenger Information System), and **MIDAS** (Migration Information and Data Analysis System) border systems to more entry points (pp. 28, 31).
  - Complete nationwide **Mining Marshals** coverage across all identified mining sites (**over 530 identified**, up from 420 protected) and resolve governance/funding questions for accountability (pp. 28, 31).
  - Deploy trained **Forest Guards, Agro-Rangers, and Mining Marshals** to protect agricultural belts, farming communities, grazing routes, forest reserves, and mineral deposits (p. 28).
  - **First 100 Days:** *"Name and open the first secured farm-to-market corridors, with published security and output data"* (p. 94).
  - Launch full implementation of the **National Protection Policy and Strategy for Critical National Assets** with real-time surveillance over energy pipelines, power grids, railways, schools, and health facilities (p. 28).
- **Domestic Defence Industrialisation & Personnel Welfare (p. 29):**
  - Expand domestic production of ammunition, personal protective gear, armoured vehicles, and tactical drones; build integrated **C3I** (command, control, communications, and intelligence) systems; deepen defence R&D, technology transfer, and private co-investment partnerships (p. 29).
  - Implement approved **multi-tier salary increases** for service personnel in full and extend them to operational allowances; expand housing, healthcare, and education support (p. 29).
  - Expedite valid insurance and pension payouts, guaranteeing permanent institutional care and educational sponsorship for families of fallen heroes (p. 29).
  - Complete rehabilitation of dilapidated police barracks and housing infrastructure; institutionalise psychological support and physical rehabilitation across all services (p. 29).
- **Correctional Reform, Fire Service & Maritime/Cyber Emergencies (p. 30):**
  - Expand non-custodial measures, cognitive rehabilitation, vocational training, and social reintegration for low-risk offenders to cut prison overcrowding (p. 30).
  - Modernise fire stations and equipment, extending fire-service coverage *"progressively towards every local government area"* (p. 30).
  - Upgrade maritime surveillance, cybersecurity defences, emergency communications networks, and a **national drone fleet** for early warning and search-and-rescue (p. 30).
- **National Identity & Data Privacy (pp. 30–31, 90, 92):**
  - Enrol **95% of citizens and legal residents** in the National Identity Database (baseline: `137,260,026` as at June 2026), deploying specialised mobile registration units for agrarian communities, older persons, and vulnerable citizens (pp. 30–31, 90).
  - Complete integration of the remaining **9 of 20 databases** and **6 of 50 MDAs** (baseline: 11 of 20 databases and 44 of 50 MDAs integrated) into the central foundational identity database (pp. 30, 90, 92).
  - Enforce data privacy protections (referencing **NDPC**, p. 107) and leverage biometric identity to curb financial fraud and target social welfare (p. 30).

### 3.2 Macroeconomics, Fiscal Policy, Taxation, Credit & Financial Markets
- **Macroeconomic Growth & Inflation Targets (pp. 18, 78–79, 94):**
  - Accelerate annual real GDP growth to **6%** (p. 18: *"Annual economic growth accelerated to 6 per cent"*). *(Compare p. 79 table: `"Catalytic Sectors Growth"` targeted at `At least 12% full-year real sector growth in 2031` from Q2 2026 baseline `9.62%`.)*
  - Reduce consumer-price headline inflation to **single digits — at most 8% year-on-year by end-2031** (*"subject to credible, independent monetary policy"*, p. 79; baseline `14.63%` in Sept 2026).
  - **External reserves:** Build gross external reserves **above US$60 billion** (p. 70: *"the baseline [US$55.25bn] for the commitment to build reserves above US$60 billion"*). Monitor both gross and net reserves, service exports, BoP composition, and legitimate FX access (p. 78).
  - **First 100 Days:** Publish first **quarterly cost-of-living basket** from day one (p. 94); publish baselines, costs, targets, and named owners for headline commitments (pp. 93–94).
- **Fiscal Consolidation, Public Audit & Taxation (pp. 78, 88–89, 93–94, 108):**
  - **First 100 Days:** *"Review the burden of multiple taxation on small businesses and households"* (p. 94) and *"Commission an independent audit of unfinished and under-used public assets and recoverable waste within the first 100 days, publish the results, and report each year what has been recovered, completed or put to use"* (pp. 88, 94).
  - Publish *"exactly how subsidy savings are lowering the cost of governance and funding development"* (p. 89).
  - Sustain implementation of the **four tax reform laws** (signed June 2025, in force since 1 Jan 2026) and the **Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS)** framework (pp. 15, 71, 84, 108).
  - *(Glossary only, p. 108):* **Tax-to-GDP ratio:** *"The manifesto plans an independent recalculation."*
- **Long-Term Capital, FDI Tracking & Consumer/MSME Credit (pp. 18, 44–45, 74–75, 78–79, 105, 108):**
  - **FDI target:** Reach **at least US$5 billion annual recorded FDI** *"with a separate audited register of productive deployment"* (p. 79; note p. 74 critique that of US$23.22bn 2025 capital importation, only US$923m was FDI). Trace every major investment commitment from approval to land, financial close, construction, commissioning, exports, and jobs (pp. 18, 78).
  - Publish prospective, consultative schedules for trade policy, investment incentives, electricity pricing, tariff changes, free-zone administration, and sector regulation (p. 78).
  - **Consumer credit (CrediCorp):** Expand structured, equitable consumer credit to **50% of economically active Nigerians by 2030** (pp. 18, 79; baseline: >350,000 Nigerians / ₦53.5bn, pp. 70–71).
  - *(Glossary only, p. 105):* CrediCorp's **S.C.A.L.E. Fund** *"is to enable up to one million credit-backed purchases from Nigerian manufacturers."*
  - *(Glossary detail, p. 108; body reference p. 44):* **YouthCred** *"to expand from just over 50,000 to one million young Nigerians"* (p. 108); enact the **Youth Bank law** and expand **NEXTGEN Bank**, **NYIF**, and **YouthCred** partner-bank lending without traditional collateral (p. 44).
  - Expand **GLOW** women's credit-guarantee window into a permanent national facility; work with **Development Bank of Nigeria (DBN)** on collateral-light lending for women entrepreneurs (p. 45).
  - Prioritise credit guarantees, factoring, export receivables finance, working-capital access, long-dated local-currency instruments, and investable concessions (pp. 75, 78).

### 3.3 Power, Clean Energy, Oil & Gas, and CNG Mobility
- **Power Generation, Transmission & Market Governance (pp. 18, 43, 58–59, 73, 79, 108):**
  - Scale available power generation to **at least 7,500 MW sustained average** and **10,000 MW peak by 2031** (pp. 18, 79; Q2 2026 baseline reported on p. 79 as 4,022 MW avg / 5,800 MW peak).
  - Strengthen coordinated federal and state electricity market development under the **Electricity Act 2023**, competitive embedded and captive generation, transparently contracted bilateral supply, and bankable distribution investment (p. 73).
  - Fast-track the **Presidential Power Initiative** (p. 73; *Glossary p. 108 specifies:* *"whose phases two and three are to be fast-tracked"*), transmission reinforcement, network automation, and industrial feeders tied to factories, agro-processing zones, tech parks, and EPZs (p. 73).
  - *(Glossary only, p. 108):* **Transmission Infrastructure Fund:** *"The fund to finance the remaining power-transmission projects."*
  - Execute commercially viable **gas-to-power** projects by resolving gas supply contracts, transportation, payment security, and dispatch constraints together—*"not by adding stranded generating assets"* (p. 73).
  - Enable dedicated, metered power for industrial clusters and free zones, with **quarterly public reporting** on average delivered MW, reliable service hours, voltage quality, and manufacturing downtime (p. 73).
- **Metering, Losses, Legacy Debts & Tariffs (pp. 18, 58–59, 73, 79):**
  - Deploy **7 million more meters, at least 2 million a year**, under the **Presidential Metering Initiative** (p. 79; also cites US$700m financing for 5m additional smart meters and **100% metering target by 2030** on p. 59).
  - Cut **ATC&C losses** to **at most 16%** by 2031 (p. 79; note p. 59 also cites *"against a 15 per cent target by 2030"*).
  - Enforce differentiated service obligations, manage GenCo legacy liabilities transparently (p. 59 notes planned ~₦730bn Series 2 bond in Q2–Q3 2026 under the ₦4tn bond framework), and protect vulnerable consumers via *"explicit, fiscally funded support"* (p. 73).
- **Clean Energy & Off-Grid Solar (pp. 18, 43, 58–59, 106–107):**
  - Bring steady solar power to **30% of health facilities by end-2027** under *Power for Health* (p. 43).
  - Extend clean off-grid power via **DARES** towards **17.5 million Nigerians** in underserved communities (pp. 58, 106).
  - *(Glossary only, p. 107):* NASENI **Gora Solar Industrial Park** noted under NASENI projects.
- **Oil & Gas Upstream/Midstream (pp. 60, 79, 106):**
  - Maintain momentum toward a medium-term crude oil output target of **3 million barrels per day** (p. 60).
  - Target a **~US$50 billion** upstream project pipeline (HA, Bonga South West, Zaba Zaba, Owowo, Nwadoro, Bosi, Preowie, Nsiko, Usan, Erha) and **US$2 billion** in midstream/downstream clean-energy prospects from 2026 onward (p. 60).
  - Complete remaining 6% of **AKK** gas pipeline (94% complete) and 2% of **OB3** gas pipeline (98% complete) (pp. 60, 105, 108). *(Glossary p. 106 also defines Host Community Development Trust).*
- **CNG & Electric Mobility (pp. 13, 61, 73):**
  - Scale the **National Affordable CNG Transit Programme (NACTP)** (₦900bn NGF ring-fenced pool across all 36 states + FCT) to deploy **3,700 CNG buses**, **55,500 CNG tricycles**, **37,000 conversion kits**, and **74 conversion centres**, targeting **~40% fare reductions** (~₦17,600/month commuter savings), **1.2 billion passenger trips/year**, **~87,500 direct jobs** (up to 150,000 direct/indirect), **35–50 MMscf/d** domestic gas demand, and displacing **1 million litres/day** of imported petrol/diesel (p. 61).
  - Phased NACTP schedule (p. 61): Kaduna/Kano pilot Oct–Dec 2026 (43 CNG buses across 13 cities, 30 buses for 10 university campuses, 600 kits, 12 stations) -> **national scale-up through 2027** -> **independent evaluation end of 2027** -> **institutionalisation in 2028**, with every asset GPS-tracked on a public register (p. 61).
  - Expand **Pi-CNG & EV** safe refuelling and conversion along freight/export haulage, agricultural logistics, and industrial corridors, clarifying that *"CNG transport must complement; not be misrepresented as; a substitute for gas-fired electricity generation"* (p. 73).

### 3.5 Industry, Manufacturing, Trade, Non-Oil Exports, Solid Minerals, Steel, Digital & Creative Economy
- **Manufacturing, Industrial Clusters & Free Zones (pp. 18, 63, 74, 77, 79, 105–107):**
  - Grow **manufacturing share of GDP** from `7.72%` (Q2 2026) to **at least 12% of GDP** (p. 79).
